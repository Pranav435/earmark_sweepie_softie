# Reading messages after the benchmark: wider rules, a message model, and owed loops that wait

Amends ADR 0017 (the fade) and ADR 0027 (rules, not a model). The accuracy benchmark of 0.1.14 (`docs/benchmark.md`) found five things worth fixing. A loop more than half an hour old almost never got a chip. Acknowledgements outside plain English opened stray loops. The word lists caught under half of urgent messages, and only 60% of messages that ask. Amounts of four or more digits lost their last digits. And quick replies offered "Yes / No" to "kahan hai tu??". Pranav chose from options: a slower fade for owed loops; rules plus a small model on the phone, kept only if it beats the rules on cases nobody tuned against; and a fresh, sealed set of benchmark cases to prove it (`docs/benchmark-v2.md`).

**Owed loops wait.** A missed call, a request for money, a bill, a reminder you set, or a message or email that asks or is urgent is **owed**. At the first unlock after it came in, its `recent` fades with a time constant of six hours instead of thirty minutes. A missed call from four hours ago scores 0.72 and gets the chip. Chatter still fades in half an hour, so "haha nice" from two hours ago stays at 0.48. Once you've had a chance to see a loop, at a later unlock, it fades in half an hour like anything else. That keeps ADR 0017's rule that an ignored loop doesn't come back at every unlock. A calendar event isn't owed, because it's over once its time has passed. At an unlock, an urgent loop above the bar comes before one that isn't urgent, as an urgent arrival already does during use (ADR 0027).

**Wider word lists.** `Urgency` reads each message once into named clues. The lists now know:

- **Not urgent:** "not urgent", "no rush", "koi jaldi nahi" and "कोई जल्दी नहीं" say it isn't. "Was urgent", "yesterday" and "sorted now" say it's over. Laughter says it's a joke. Words in quotes are someone else's, and a reaction such as Liked “are you coming?” asks nothing.
- **Urgent:** urgent words in Marathi, Gujarati, Tamil, Bengali, Arabic, Spanish, German and other testers' languages, plus "come now", "everyone's waiting", "kahan hai tu", "phone kyu nahi utha raha", and a request written in capitals.
- **Asks:** questions without a question mark: a question word at the start of a clause, Hinglish and Hindi question words ("kab", "kidhar", "कब"), and "kya" at the end. It also counts instructions aimed at you ("bring the charger", "ghar aate waqt bread le aana", "शाम को कॉल करना"). An offer ("lmk if you need anything") doesn't ask, and neither does a question taken back with "nvm".
- **Acknowledgements:** words in many more languages, and filler that rides along ("thanks bhai", "ठीक है बेटा", "Danke dir"). Reactions written with straight quotes count, and so does "Liked an image".

**The message model.** Beside the lists, `MessageModel` reads every message with three logistic regressions, for urgent, asks and acknowledges. It reads hashed words, word pairs, runs of two to four letters, emoji and punctuation, and which clues fired. It learned from 2,644 labelled messages in `model/data`, written for it from `benchmark/LABELS.md` and never shown the benchmark's cases. Five-fold cross-validation picks each head's two thresholds: how sure it must be to say yes when the lists say no, and to say no when they say yes. The lists that say what a message *isn't* have the last word, because a model of words can't tell what "not" applies to. These are "not urgent", "it's over", a reaction, an offer, and, for acknowledgements, any question, request or greeting. The weights are one byte each, 96 KB in the APK, loaded from a Java resource on the engine's thread when the listener connects. Without them the lists decide alone, and the log says "message model missing". The log line for a loop lists "model" among its reasons when the model overruled the lists, still never the words. ADR 0027 chose rules partly because the A34 has no on-device language model. This one is ours, runs the same on every phone, needs no download, and keeps `INTERNET` out of the manifest (ADR 0002).

**The classifier reads more.** Bills arrive in Hindi, Marathi and Hinglish ("देय है", "tak due hai"), as fees and maintenance, as credit card dues, and as emails. A failed charge, a bounced EMI or a trip that couldn't be charged is owed like a bill. A bank's debit text ("Sent Rs.450.00 … To ISHAAN") is evidence that you paid. A ride confirmation that carries a ride code counts as booked, not as a login code. Delivery tracking doesn't count as booked. Payment requests worded "Payment request from Aisha" or "sent you a reminder to pay" are recognised, and so are carriers' missed-call texts. A spam caller opens nothing, and neither does a phishing text from an unsaved number. A business bot on WhatsApp ("Reply 'Menu'") is a text from a business. An email's quoted reply and forwarded mail aren't its sender asking. Calendar invitations, document comments, newsletters and sales pitches aren't people waiting on you. Your name counts written in an Indian script ("प्रणव"), but not followed by someone else's initial ("Pranav K").

**Amounts** read the whole number, with Indian and European grouping, currency written after it ("500 rs", "15,50 €"), and lakh, crore and k. Rupees are shown the Indian way, "₹1,25,000".

**Quick replies** answer the question asked: "On my way" to where are you, "All good, you?" to how are you, "Let me check" to what and when, "Okay, will do" to a request, "Same to you!" to festival wishes. A message in Hindi or Hinglish gets its replies in Hindi or Hinglish ("Raste mein hoon", "हाँ"). Those replies avoid verbs that say whether you're a man or a woman.

**Consequences:**

- On the sealed held-out cases the classifier is right 91% of the time (82% before), urgent 92% (83%), asks 93% (69%), and the chip at unlock 96% (46%). The model adds 1.6 points on urgent and 2.6 on asks over the wider lists alone. `docs/benchmark-v2.md` has every number.
- Reading costs about twice what it did: about 1.1 ms a notification on the Galaxy A34 in a debug build (0.6 ms before), and 20 ms for the first one while the weights load.
- More chips for old owed things at the first unlock after a long time away. The three-ignores mute still limits each kind.
- The held-out set is spent: rules changed from here on will be tuned with it in view. The next benchmark needs a new one.
- Retraining is `./gradlew :core:trainModel` (model/README.md). A change to the clue names changes the model's features, so it needs a retrain.
